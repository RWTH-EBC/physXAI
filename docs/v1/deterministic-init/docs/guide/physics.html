PINN: physics targets

v = px.Vars()
physics = v.u_hp * 10000 * (v.T_room + 15 - v.T_amb) / ((v.T_room + 15) * 0.55)
prep = PreprocessingSingleStep(inputs=[...], output='P_el', columns={'pinn': physics})
m = PINNModel(physics_targets={'pinn': 0.5})
td = prep.pipeline(path, model=m)
m.pipeline(td)

The loss is mse(ŷ, y)/s + Σ w_i · mse(ŷ, p_i)/s with s = var(y_train), see Models. Physics targets are taken at the predicted time; for a step model of Change(T), shift the column by one step (columns={'dT': step.lag(1)}).

Physics residuals with trainable parameters

A residual r(x, y; θ) that should be zero, written with px.Prediction() (the output of the model) and trainable parameters, can be added to every single step keras model:

from physXAI.models.ann.pinn.physics import PhysicsResidual

dt = 900.0
v = px.Vars()
tau, K_u = px.positive(20 * 3600.0, 'tau'), px.positive(20.0, 'K_u')
K_0 = px.Param(0.0, name='K_0')
dT = (1 - px.exp(-dt / tau)) * (v.T_amb + K_u * v.u_hp + K_0 - v.T)       # 1R1C, exact zero order hold

m = ClassicalANNModel(physics=PhysicsResidual(px.Prediction() - dT, weight=0.5, name='rc'),
                      physics_fit='calibrate')
td = prep.pipeline(path, model=m)   # the columns of the residual become loss columns
m.pipeline(td)
m.physics_parameters                # {'tau': ..., 'K_u': ..., 'K_0': ..., 'log_tau': ..., ...}

  • px.positive(value, name) is value · exp(p) with a trainable p = 0: positive, scaled by the physical initial value, changed relatively by the training. px.Param is a plain (signed) parameter.

  • The residual is evaluated with the prediction (r(x, ŷ), the physics term of the network) and with the measured target (r(x, y), the fit of the physical model to the data), with shared parameters.

  • physics_fit='calibrate' (default): the parameters are first fitted to the data (grey-box identification), then fixed while the network is trained. When parameters are trained together with the network only through the physics term, they follow the network and lose their physical meaning. 'joint' trains both together (including the fit term), 'fixed' keeps the initial values.

  • The calibration is a nonlinear least squares problem in the few parameters, min Σ r(x, y; θ)² on the training data. It is solved deterministically with a trust region method (scipy.optimize.least_squares) and the exact Jacobian of the residual (CasADi), independent of the seed, the batch order and a learning rate, and converged to the minimum (gradient descent with early stopping stops somewhere in the flat valley of correlated parameters, so its estimates depend on the seed). td.physics['calibration_record'] holds the result of the solver. Residuals without CasADi form (e.g. a px.Param without initial value) are calibrated by gradient descent (physics_learning_rate).

  • History: data_loss, <name>_loss, <name>_fit_loss; results in td.physics (parameters, fit per split, history of the calibration). Only parameters are trainable in residuals, no blocks.

  • The variables of the residual are data columns at the time of the inputs; px.Target(name) is a column at the predicted time. They do not have to be inputs of the model (the MPC never sees them): only the network is saved and exported.

  • Discretize physical models exactly over the time step (zero order hold), an explicit Euler step is inaccurate and unstable for time constants of the order of the time step. Only the time constant and steady state gains are identifiable from temperatures, not R and C separately. Data recorded under feedback control correlate the control with the temperatures, which correlates the estimates.

  • With RecursiveTraining, the residual acts in the single step phase; the multi step phase uses the error of the recursive prediction.

See examples/09_physics_residual.py.

Physically consistent neural network (PCNN)

Following Di Natale et al. (2022), Physically Consistent Neural Networks for building thermal modeling: Theory and analysis, Applied Energy 325, 119806, the temperature is the sum of an energy accumulator E and the unforced dynamics D:

E(k+1) = E(k) + Σ a·u_heating(k) − Σ d·u_cooling(k) + Σ b·(T_x(k) − T(k))
D(k+1) = D(k) + f(D(k), x(k))          T = D + E

f is a recurrent network on D and the remaining inputs x (e.g. solar radiation), which excludes the controls and the temperatures T_x. Because D does not depend on them,

∂T(k+i)/∂u(k+i−j) = (1 − Σ b)^(j−1) · a > 0

for any network: the predicted temperature rises with heating and with T_x and falls with cooling over the whole horizon, also on unseen inputs. The parameters are positive by construction (s = s₀ · s̃, s̃ ≥ 0, starting at 1), 1 − Σ b > 0 is checked after the training (consistency_margin).

from physXAI.models.ann.ann_design import PCNNModel

prep = PreprocessingMultiStep(inputs=['oveHeaPumY_u', 'weaSta_reaWeaTDryBul_y', 'weaSta_reaWeaHDirNor_y', 'reaTZon_y'],
                              output=px.Change('reaTZon_y'), warmup_width='3h', label_width='12h', eval_horizon='24h')
m = PCNNModel(heating={'oveHeaPumY_u': 0.5},              # +1 K in 2 h at full power: 0.5 K/h
              losses={'weaSta_reaWeaTDryBul_y': 0.01},    # -1.5 K in 6 h at 25 K colder outside: 0.01 1/h
              rnn_layer='LSTM', rnn_units=16)
m.pipeline(prep.pipeline(path))
td.physics['parameters']        # trained rates per hour and the consistency margin

  • The initial rates are rules of thumb per hour (heating, cooling: K/h per unit of the input; losses: 1/h), they are scaled to the time step.

  • T is an input and fed back (closed loop); the model warms up on the measured past, as in AgentLib-MPC, and is trained on the trajectory over label_width.

  • The saved step model [sequence, E, *states of f] → [ΔT, E, *states of f] consists of layers AgentLib-MPC translates to CasADi; it is used like an RNN.

  • Consistency only holds for the inputs of the physics module (paper, section 6.3), e.g. not for solar gains processed by f. Time features (time of day, month) have to be data columns.

See examples/10_pcnn.py.
