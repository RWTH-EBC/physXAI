Models

All models share model.pipeline(td): build, train, evaluate. The trained model is returned and kept as model.trained_model, Run.save stores it with its configs.

Single step models

Predict the next time step (shift=1), or an output at the time of the inputs (shift=0). Applied recursively in the MPC when they predict one of their inputs. The keras networks have one output; train one model per output.

Model

Description

LinearRegressionModel()

linear regression (scikit-learn, saved as joblib)

ClassicalANNModel(n_layers, n_neurons, activation_function)

feed forward network

CMNNModel(monotonies, activation_split)

constrained monotonic neural network

RBFModel(n_neurons)

radial basis function network

LinANNModel()

linear regression plus an RBF network on its residuals

PINNModel(physics_targets)

physics-informed loss on physics targets, see below

ModularANN(architecture, solver='adam', 'ols' or 'qp')

any expression graph, see Expressions

ModularANN(px.Linear(terms), solver='qp', shape=ShapeConstraints(...))

constrained regression, see below

Keras models share the training options epochs, learning_rate, batch_size, early_stopping_epochs and random_seed. Early stopping needs validation data. random_seed seeds the weight initialization and the batch order; it defaults to the global seed (physXAI.set_seed), else a new seed is drawn and stored in the config. Unknown arguments raise a TypeError.

Constrained regression

A linear regression on physical terms whose prediction is monotonic or convex in raw data columns, guaranteed on a domain. The fit is a convex quadratic program: one global optimum, no seeds.

from physXAI.models import ModularANN, ShapeConstraints

v = px.Vars()
u, T, T_amb = v.oveHeaPumY_u, v.reaTZon_y, v.weaSta_reaWeaTDryBul_y
model = ModularANN(
    px.Linear([u, u * T_amb, u * T, T_amb]),
    solver="qp",
    shape=ShapeConstraints(monotonicities={"oveHeaPumY_u": 1, "weaSta_reaWeaTDryBul_y": -1},
                           bounds={"weaSta_reaWeaTDryBul_y": (250.0, 310.0)}),
)
td = prep.pipeline(path, model=model)
model.pipeline(td)
td.physics["shape_constraints"]     # certificates, violations, binding share, cost of the physics

  • Constraints on raw columns: monotonicities={'x': 1 | -1} constrains dy/dx of the whole model, e.g. d/dT_amb (w1 · u · T_amb + w2 · T_amb) = w1 · u + w2, which depends on u. convexities={'x': 1 | -1} constrains d²y/dx² (convex or concave along x, not the joint convexity in several columns). Every column is constrained separately, also lags (u and u_lag1).

  • Sign of single weights: px.Linear(terms, monotonicities={'term': 1}) bounds the weight of a term; this holds globally and is also kept by recursive_training.

  • Domain: domain='box' (default) enforces the constraints on the box of the training data, or on bounds (e.g. the bounds of the MPC). The derivative is linear in the weights, so this is a semi-infinite program, solved with the exchange method: the least squares problem on a finite set of constraint points, the most violated points of the box (Sobol sample, local minimization) are added until no violation is left. domain='data' only enforces them on the training samples, e.g. when a physical relation only holds in the operating range of the data (more cold supply air only cools while T_in < T).

  • Certificates per constraint: exact (the derivative is multilinear in its columns, e.g. linear, bilinear and quadratic terms: its minimum over the box lies in a corner, and all corners are constraint points, so the constraint holds on the whole box), verified (no violation found on the box), data (holds on the training samples), violated (no convergence after max_iter, with warning).

  • Diagnosis: the train RMSE with and without the constraints (the cost of the physics) and the share of training samples where a constraint binds: constraints binding on many samples show where the data contradicts the physics. The report contains a table of it.

  • Numerics: terms and target are standardized, the least squares problem is solved in the coordinates of its QR decomposition (no squared condition number), a tiny ridge (ridge=1e-10) makes the solution unique for collinear terms. Terms that are not defined everywhere in the box (e.g. a division by zero) are left out at those points with a warning; restrict the box with bounds.

  • shape cannot be combined with recursive_training (gradient descent would not keep it) or a physics residual. Closed form solvers (ols, qp) have no online_pipeline: train a new model on the combined data.

  • The model is exported like any modular model: the keras model of the terms with one dense layer.

See examples/05_constrained_regression.py.

Physics-informed neural networks

A physics target is a data column computed from a physical model of the output. The network keeps one output; the physics targets only enter the loss:

v = px.Vars()
physics = v.u_hp * 10000 * (v.T_room + 15 - v.T_amb) / ((v.T_room + 15) * 0.55)
prep = PreprocessingSingleStep(inputs=[...], output='P_el', columns={'pinn': physics})
m = PINNModel(physics_targets={'pinn': 0.5})
td = prep.pipeline(path, model=m)     # the model adds its physics targets to the loss columns
m.pipeline(td)

  • Loss: mse(ŷ, y)/s + Σ w_i · mse(ŷ, p_i)/s with the characteristic scale s = var(y_train) (normalize_terms=True). The data term is 1 − R² on the training data, the weight w_i the relative importance of the physics. The terms are not averaged, adding a physics term does not change the weight of the data.

  • The weight encodes the trust in the physical model and is chosen by validation (e.g. the error over the horizon, see Comparing models). Adaptive weighting schemes (e.g. GradNorm, learning rate annealing) target PDE residuals of very different magnitudes and are not used.

  • Early stopping monitors the validation data loss (early_stopping_on='data'), so the selected model does not depend on the physics weights; 'total' monitors the weighted loss.

  • History and report hold every term (data_loss, <target>_loss); td.metrics.physics the disagreement with each physics target per split.

  • Physics targets are taken at the predicted time, like the output. For Change(T) and a physical model of the step T(t) → T(t+1) computed from the data at t, shift the column: columns={'dT': step.lag(1)}.

  • The saved model has one output and is exported to AgentLib-MPC like any other model.

Multi step models

RNNModel(rnn_layer='RNN' | 'GRU' | 'LSTM', rnn_units) predicts a trajectory, see Recurrent models. PCNNModel(heating, cooling, losses) is a physically consistent recurrent model, see Physics-informed models.

Every single step keras model takes a physics residual with trainable physical parameters (physics=PhysicsResidual(...)), see Physics-informed models.

Saving and loading

run.save("model", prep, model, td)                       # recommended
model.pipeline(td, save_path="results/x/model")      # only the model file and its report
trained = model.load_model("results/x/model.keras")
model = AbstractModel.model_from_config(json.load(open("results/x/model_model.json")))

online_pipeline(td, load_path) loads a saved model and trains it further on new data.

Reports

pipeline does not open plots. model.plot(td) returns the report as plotly figure, pipeline(td, show_plot=True) opens it, and Run.save writes it as <name>.html.
